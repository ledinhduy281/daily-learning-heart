# Example briefing: On-treatment systolic blood pressure targets and tolerability

**Pillar 1: Hypertension and cardiovascular risk factors.** This abridged English example illustrates the structure of an educational briefing. It compares historical publications, does not claim to be the latest guidance, and does not record a live verification run. Use the [editorial prompt](PROMPT.md) when drafting a full, sourced briefing.

## Block A — One focused guideline recommendation

**Primary guideline (ESC, 2024): [2024 ESC Guidelines for the management of elevated blood pressure and hypertension](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehae178).** The [2018 ESC/ESH guideline](https://doi.org/10.1093/eurheartj/ehy339) provides historical context only.

The 2024 document uses an elevated blood pressure category to reflect that cardiovascular risk does not start at the diagnostic threshold for hypertension. The comparison should distinguish three questions: how blood pressure is classified, when treatment starts, and what target applies once treatment begins. The 2024 guideline's systolic target of 120–129 mmHg for many treated adults is conditional on tolerability; it is not a target to apply to every patient regardless of symptoms or frailty. Read the original recommendation and its exceptions together.

## Block B — Landmark evidence for intensive blood-pressure control

| Study | Recommendation it helps explain | Population | What not to extrapolate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [SPRINT](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1511939), *NEJM* (2015), landmark RCT | Supports more intensive on-treatment systolic lowering when tolerated; serious hypotension and acute kidney injury were more frequent in the intensive arm. | Adults at elevated cardiovascular risk without diabetes; compared systolic targets below 120 and below 140 mmHg. | The trial targets do not equal the guideline's 120–129 mmHg range. Do not assume direct applicability to people with diabetes or prior stroke, who were excluded. |

In a full briefing, report each included study's design, eligibility, comparator, primary endpoint, effect estimate, confidence interval, follow-up, and key harms as reported. This short example omits numerical trial results rather than presenting unsourced estimates.

## Take-home teaching points

- **[Guideline evolution]** Separate a diagnosis threshold from a treatment threshold and a treatment target.
- **[Guideline evolution]** Read tolerability and exceptions alongside the target.
- **[Landmark trial]** Check who was enrolled and how blood pressure and outcomes were measured before comparing results.

Cross-check this material against the original sources and current guidance before using it in teaching or clinical decisions.
